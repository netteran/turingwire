In a notable instance of parallel research, two teams independently addressed the same open quantum cryptography problem utilizing OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra, submitting their findings just three hours apart. This occurrence highlights the increasing reliance on advanced AI models for tackling complex scientific challenges. One researcher remarked on the trend, stating that the first step in addressing an open problem is often to consult GPT for potential solutions.

The simultaneous submissions prompt a critical examination of the concept of 'independent discovery' in the context of AI-assisted research. As researchers increasingly turn to the same generative models, the implications for originality and innovation in scientific inquiry become more complex. The article emphasizes the need for a nuanced understanding of how AI tools influence the research landscape, particularly in fields like quantum cryptography where rapid advancements are crucial.

This case serves as a compelling example of the intersection between AI capabilities and scientific problem-solving, raising important questions about the nature of collaboration and competition in research.
