## Problem
This work addresses the gap in quantitative prediction of peptide–protein interactions, particularly in mapping binding interfaces and estimating affinities. The authors highlight the need for a model that integrates both sequence and structural information, which is not sufficiently covered in existing literature. The paper is a preprint and has not undergone peer review.

## Method
The authors introduce VITAL, a dual-channel deep learning framework that co-learns sequence and structural contexts. The architecture leverages two input channels: one for peptide sequences and another for protein structures, allowing the model to capture complex interactions. The training process involves optimizing a loss function that balances the prediction of interaction affinities and the identification of binding interfaces. Specific details regarding the dataset, training compute, and hyperparameters are not disclosed in the available text.

The available text does not report quantitative results.

## Limitations
The authors acknowledge that the model's performance may be limited by the quality and diversity of the training data. Additionally, the lack of peer review may indicate that the findings are preliminary and require further validation. The model's generalizability to diverse peptide–protein pairs is also not fully established.

## Why it matters
The implications of this work are significant for the fields of computational biology and drug discovery, as accurate predictions of peptide–protein interactions can facilitate the design of novel therapeutics. The integration of sequence and structural data in VITAL may set a precedent for future models in this domain, as published in [Nature Machine Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01291-z).
