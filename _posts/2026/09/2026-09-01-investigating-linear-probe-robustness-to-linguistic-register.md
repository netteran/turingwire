  "body": "## Problem\nThis work addresses the gap in understanding the generalizability of linear probes trained on large language models (LLMs) in medical question-answering (QA) tasks. Prior research has shown conflicting results regarding the robustness of these probes across different input shifts, but the lack of clarity stems from confounding variables in cross-dataset evaluations. This paper isolates three specific variables: writing style (register), medical specialty, and corpus (dataset), to provide a clearer analysis of linear probe performance.\n\n## Method\nThe authors constructed a benchmark comprising 500 MedQA entries, each rewritten into four distinct styles: textbook, patient, clinical note, and colloquial. Each entry is annotated with its clinical specialty and grouped with two additional exam corpora, MedMCQA and MMLU-medical, for cross-dataset evaluation. The study probes four open-weight LLMs ranging from 2 to 8 billion parameters. The evaluation focuses on the stability of the truth direction in hidden state space, measured by AUROC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve) across the different conditions.\n\n##
